Cover, in plain language

How battery cover is designed to work.

The idea is straightforward: understand the battery and the risks, make any contribution clear, and give a rider a defined way to ask for help if a covered event happens.

The proposed journey

From getting started to getting support.

This describes the planned service in the VoltShield blueprint. It is not yet a live insurance or payment service.

01
Before you join

See what is covered and what it may cost.

A rider should receive clear cover terms, exclusions, contribution amounts, claim conditions and insurer details before agreeing. The estimate on this site is only an illustration, not a quote or offer.

02
With your permission

Share only the information needed.

If a future product uses battery or riding data, the rider should actively opt in and be told what is collected and why. The blueprint calls for protecting precise location and separating driving information appropriately. You should be able to understand the choice before making it.

03
At a participating swap station

Check the battery and contribution.

The proposed system would identify the battery and connect a swap event to the pricing service. Battery use and condition could help estimate risk; the contribution should be shown clearly before any payment is requested.

04
If payment is enabled

Confirm a payment through M-Pesa.

The blueprint proposes an M-Pesa Daraja prompt at the point of a swap. A rider should see and approve the actual amount on the handset. Never share an M-Pesa PIN with a person or enter it into this website.

05
If something goes wrong

Ask for help and have the event reviewed.

A rider would contact the insurer or support team using the published claim route. Battery health, incident details and policy terms would be checked to decide whether the event qualifies. A USSD menu could offer basic account or battery information, but does not itself approve a claim.

What the blueprint is exploring

Three different battery and vehicle risks.

These are proposed risk categories, not confirmation that any specific incident is covered. The final insurer policy would define the actual benefits, limits, waiting periods and exclusions.

01 / Impact

Serious collision damage

The blueprint models a severe road impact that damages the motorcycle and battery casing. A policy would need to define the eligible damage and evidence required.

02 / Battery health

Early battery degradation

The blueprint studies battery State of Health falling below 70% before 1,200 standard cycles. Actual measurement, eligibility and any replacement benefit must be specified by an insurer.

03 / Thermal event

Thermal runaway or fire

The blueprint includes catastrophic cell failure or fire as a separate risk. Any protection, safety response and claim process would depend on final policy wording.

Pay-as-you-swap

A small payment linked to a battery swap.

The proposed Pay-As-You-Swap model would collect a clearly disclosed cover contribution alongside a battery swap, rather than asking every rider for a large annual payment up front. The blueprint describes KES 18–36 per swap as an indicative target; its own high-risk benchmark lists KES 38.40. Those figures need reconciliation and insurer approval.

For now, no M-Pesa charge is made by this website. The estimate is only an example; it does not activate cover, create an insurance policy, or guarantee a future price.
